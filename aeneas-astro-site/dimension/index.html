Intro

I'm a recent Physics graduate from the University of Connecticut, preparing to appy to graduate programs in astronomy and astrophysics for the Fall 2027 semester. My research focuses on star formation in nearby galaxies, using multi-wavelength survey data to understand how gas converts into stars across different galactic environments.

I worked as part of the PHANGS collaboration with Dr. Chris Faesi during my undergraduate career, studying the Kennicutt–Schmidt star formation relation at ~100 pc resolution in the nearby spiral galaxy NGC 1566. You can read more about this work in Research and Publications below.

Research

Star Formation in NGC 1566

My primary project examined the Kennicutt–Schmidt (KS) star formation relation — the empirical link between gas surface density and star formation rate — at ~100 pc resolution in the nearby spiral galaxy NGC 1566. The analysis combines dust-corrected Hα emission from PHANGS–MUSE (tracing recent star formation) with CO(2–1) emission from PHANGS–ALMA (tracing molecular gas), resolved on a pixel-by-pixel basis across different galactic environments (e.g. spiral arms, bar, disk).

We used a Bayesian hierarchical MCMC regression fitting model (LinMix, Kelly 2007) that accounts for per-pixel measurement errors, which is more statistically robust than an ordinary least-squares fit for this kind of astronomical data.

Extension: Toomre Q Stability

I'm currently extending this analysis to incorporate the Toomre Q stability parameter, which characterizes a disk's local resistance to gravitational collapse. This involves deriving a rotation curve for NGC 1566 from scratch using tilted-ring fitting methods applied to PHANGS–ALMA moment maps, then connecting environment-resolved Q profiles back to the KS-law results.
