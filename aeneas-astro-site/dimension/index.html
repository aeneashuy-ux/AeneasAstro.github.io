Research

Star Formation in NGC 1566

My primary project examined the Kennicutt–Schmidt (KS) star formation relation — the empirical link between gas surface density and star formation rate — at ~100 pc resolution in the nearby spiral galaxy NGC 1566.

The analysis combines dust-corrected Hα emission from PHANGS–MUSE (tracing recent star formation) with CO(2–1) emission from PHANGS–ALMA (tracing molecular gas), resolved on a pixel-by-pixel basis across different galactic environments including spiral arms, the bar, and disk.

We used a Bayesian hierarchical MCMC regression fitting model (LinMix, Kelly 2007) that accounts for per-pixel measurement errors, making it more statistically robust than an ordinary least-squares fit for this type of astronomical analysis.

Extension: Toomre Q Stability

I'm currently extending this analysis to incorporate the Toomre Q stability parameter, which characterizes a disk's local resistance to gravitational collapse. This involves deriving a rotation curve for NGC 1566 using tilted-ring fitting methods applied to PHANGS–ALMA moment maps and connecting environment-resolved Q profiles back to the KS-law results.
